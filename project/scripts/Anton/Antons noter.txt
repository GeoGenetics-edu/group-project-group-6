3. Alpha diversity — Shannon
Done:

estimate_richness(ps_genus, measures = "Shannon")
Boxplot via plot_richness() + geom_boxplot()
Wilcoxon test: W = 30327, p = 3.6 × 10⁻¹⁰
Visual: T2D median ≈ 1.72, Healthy median ≈ 1.54
Why these choices:

Decision	Defense
Shannon over Observed	OTU table is relative abundance (range 0–0.9), not counts — estimate_richness() errors on Observed with non-integer data. Observed on genus-level RA is also uninformative (capped at 58). Shannon integrates richness + evenness and works on any non-negative data.
Wilcoxon over t-test	Microbiome data violates all three t-test assumptions: zero inflation, right skew, non-normality. Per Alberdi lecture §2, this is the canonical justification for rank-based tests.
Result direction: T2D > Healthy — opposite of the "dysbiosis lowers diversity" intuition. T2D literature is mixed; metformin, diet, and geography can all drive higher diversity.

4. Three alpha metrics — robustness triangulation
Done:

Computed Shannon, Simpson, Inverse Simpson together.
Wilcoxon for each:
Metric	W	p-value
Shannon	30327	3.6 × 10⁻¹⁰
Simpson	30178	7.6 × 10⁻¹⁰
InvSimpson	30178	7.6 × 10⁻¹⁰
Faceted boxplot for all three.
Why: Each metric sits at a different point on the rare-to-dominant spectrum. Shannon is balanced; Simpson and InvSimpson are weighted toward dominant taxa. Agreement across all three = robust finding driven by both rare and common taxa. Identical Simpson/InvSimpson W is expected — they're monotonic transforms, so rank-based tests see them as the same.

5. Depth confounding check
Done:

cor.test(Nr..reads.sequenced, Shannon, method = "spearman") → ρ = -0.0004, p = 0.99
Scatter plot, log-x scale, colored by group, smoothed line.
Why: Diversity metrics — especially count-sensitive ones — can inflate with deeper sequencing just by chance (more rare taxa detected). If T2D samples were sequenced deeper than Healthy, the apparent diversity difference could be artefactual. The Alberdi lecture and teacher's reference Rmd both explicitly recommend this check.

Result: depth has literally zero correlation with Shannon. The diversity difference is not a depth artifact.

6. Effect size
Done:

rank_biserial(Shannon ~ Disease.MESH.ID) → r = 0.36, 95% CI [0.26, 0.45] — medium effect.
Why: With N = 432, even tiny biological differences become statistically significant. The p-value tells you the difference is real; effect size tells you whether it's meaningful. Rank-biserial is the correct effect-size measure for Wilcoxon (rank-based, no distributional assumption).

7. Beta diversity — Bray-Curtis
Done:

Transformed to relative abundance: transform_sample_counts(ps_genus, function(x) x / sum(x)).
Distance: bray_dist <- distance(ps_rel, method = "bray").
PCoA: PCoA1 explains 28.9%, PCoA2 explains 17.7% (46.6% combined).
Plot with stat_ellipse() for 95% group ellipses.
Why these choices:

Bray-Curtis is the teacher's reference standard. It's sensitive to abundance differences (vs. Jaccard which is membership-only).
PCoA projects the distance matrix into Euclidean 2D so patterns become visible.
Re-normalized to RA even though the data is already RA — protects against any per-sample numerical drift.
Visual observations:

Heavy ellipse overlap — disease alone doesn't cleanly separate communities.
A distinct bottom-left cluster, mostly T2D — suspicious for cohort/subtype effect.
Healthy ellipse appears more elongated than T2D ellipse — possible dispersion difference.
8. PERMANOVA + betadisper
Done:

Test	Statistic	p-value	Meaning
adonis2(bray_dist ~ Disease.MESH.ID)	F = 13.1, R² = 0.030	0.001	Centroids differ
betadisper(bray_dist, group)	F = 21.8	4.0 × 10⁻⁶	Dispersions also differ
Why both: PERMANOVA tests centroid separation in distance space, but a significant PERMANOVA can sometimes reflect unequal within-group spread rather than a true location shift. betadisper is the diagnostic — always paired.

Interpretation:

Disease explains only 3% of total community variation (R² = 0.030) — small but real.
Dispersions also differ → cannot cleanly attribute the PERMANOVA result to centroid shift alone.
Biological framing: T2D communities are both compositionally distinct AND more variable across patients. This is the "Anna Karenina principle" of dysbiosis — healthy guts are alike, dysbiotic guts each in their own way. Defensible biological angle, not a flaw.
9. Cohort confounding — the critical finding
Done: Cross-tabulation table(Project.ID, Disease.MESH.ID):

Project	T2D	Healthy
PRJEB1786	54	0
PRJNA299502	19	0
PRJNA422434	187	172
Then stratified PERMANOVA with strata = Project.ID: F = 13.1, R² = 0.030, p = 0.008.

Why this matters:

Two of three projects are case-only — no healthy controls in PRJEB1786 or PRJNA299502.
73 T2D samples (~28%) come from cohorts where disease and project effects are perfectly confounded.
The real case-control comparison comes from a single project (PRJNA422434).
strata = Project.ID permutes within projects — but in case-only projects, all labels are identical, so swapping does nothing. The test effectively only runs within PRJNA422434.
Even so, the within-project disease signal survives (p = 0.008 vs unstratified 0.001). Weaker, but real.
Oral exam framing: "We discovered early that two of three contributing studies were case-only, creating severe cohort/disease confounding. We addressed this via stratified PERMANOVA, which restricts permutation tests to within-cohort comparisons. The disease signal survived stratification (p = 0.008), demonstrating that our finding is not purely a study-effect artifact."
